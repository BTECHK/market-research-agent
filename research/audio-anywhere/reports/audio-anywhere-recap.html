Market recap · iOS long-document reader · 25 Sep 2026

Audio Anywhere: don't build it as described. Test one narrow version first.

Audiobook-style listening for long documents is a real complaint. But the product as described already exists, the free built-in readers are catching up, and on iOS the "read anything" phase can't be built. One niche survives: a private reader for long, structured work documents.

6 research streams + strategy pass~29K words of evidenceSources checked: medium–high confidence
Bottom line

Stop the build. Spend 2–4 weeks and under $1.5K testing a "private briefing reader" for professionals. Build only if people pay.

  1. People do complain, but mostly not the ones who'd pay. The loudest complainers use free accessibility features. We found no first-hand complaint from a lawyer, consultant or analyst.
  2. The App Store is saturated. Speechify leads on distribution, ElevenReader leads on voice quality with 10 free hours a month, and Audeus already sells Google Docs + PDF + saved progress.
  3. It's a small-business opportunity, not a venture one. The realistic outcome is about $53–106K in year 1 after Apple's cut and $0.35–0.7M a year by year 3 in the success case.
  4. The only open lane is a narrow one: accurate reading of complex document structure, playback that never stops mid-document, and on-device privacy, all for long, confidential work documents.
  5. Update after your follow-up questions: the voice is cheap and ads are what make these apps expensive. A free, no-ads app that runs on the phone, with a $2.99/mo tier, is viable and costs you almost nothing per user. Getting found is still the hard part. See the follow-up section.

Key findings

Six findings, with the evidence behind each

The pain is real and has lasted a decade. The people complaining just aren't the ones likely to pay.

  • Evidence15+ Apple Community threads from iOS 8 to iOS 26 report Speak Screen and Listen to Page stopping, skipping or looping. "iOS 18: 'Speak Screen' is largely broken".
  • EvidencePaying users report the same failure: ElevenReader "loses its place"; in Speechify, one wrong tap "goes back to the beginning of the book".
  • EvidencePDF clutter is a real problem:
    "confusing… to have the headers and footers read in the middle of the sentence" (W3C list)
  • RiskThe loudest group, blind and low-vision users, pushed back hard on subscriptions. Voice Dream had to reverse its 2024 switch to one.
  • GapWe found no first-hand evidence from lawyers, consultants or analysts, the very segment we recommend. Reddit was blocked from this environment.

Your first version is already on the market. Voice quality isn't a way to stand out anymore, because it's nearly free.

  • EvidenceAudeus markets "Read Aloud PDFs, Gdocs & more" with automatic place-saving at $119/yr.
  • EvidenceSpeechify has been profitable for 4.5 years, claims 50–60M users, and makes about $2M a month from US iOS alone (Sensor Tower). It tests about 1,300 AI-made ads a day.
  • EvidenceElevenReader gives 10 free hours a month, then $99/yr, and its parent ElevenLabs is valued at $11B.
  • OpeningNobody does both reliable long listening and accurate reading of complex document structure. Speechify's billing anger (F rating at the BBB) makes honest billing a selling point.

"Read anything from anywhere" can't be built on iOS. Apple and Google give away good-enough versions of both phases.

  • EvidenceiOS keeps apps sealed off from each other, with no third-party access to other apps' screens or to iMessage and SMS (Apple Platform Security). The most you can do is the Share Sheet, a Safari extension, screenshot text recognition and Shortcuts, and competitors already ship all of those.
  • RiskThe free iOS 26 Accessibility Reader reads any app aloud. Google Docs "Listen" has used Gemini voices since August 2025.
  • DoImport Google Docs through the file picker, which only sees files the user chooses (the drive.file scope) and avoids a $500–4.5K+ yearly security audit. Build a Chrome extension before a Mac app. If "read anything" matters, it's an Android product.

Voice costs are manageable. What really limits you is customer acquisition cost against lifetime value.

  • EvidenceA 20-page brief (~60K characters) costs about $0.90 through OpenAI or Google's standard voices, $6 through ElevenLabs v3, and $0 with the on-device Kokoro-82M voice model.
  • RiskAt $32–50 lifetime value per payer and 3–6% of installs converting, you can afford only about $1–3 per install. Speechify's ad spending pushes the cost of the main search terms well above that, so paid acquisition is out.
  • EarningsYear 1 won't replace a salary. Year 3 can pay one part-time founder in the base case, or one to three people in the success case.
  • CorrectedThe source check found a math error in the raw data: 5–8 cloud hours a month costs about $7.70–12.30, not about $2. The allowance was cut to 1–2 hours.

The window for a broad reader has closed. A narrow one has roughly 6–12 months.

  • TailwindNeural voices now pass blind tests: 61% of listeners in Edison 2026 thought AI narration was human. Costs have collapsed, and Pocket's July 2025 shutdown left its users looking for a replacement.
  • TailwindWispr raised money at a $2B valuation even though dictation is built into iOS, so a focused voice app can win next to a free built-in feature.
  • HeadwindThe Wispr comparison has a weak spot: people dictate dozens of times a day but listen to long documents 1–3 times a week. Less frequent use means weaker habits and more cancellations.
  • RiskApple adds better voices and saved progress to Accessibility Reader in iOS 27 or 28, or professionals decide they only want summaries, which NotebookLM and Docs already give free.

Costs, business models, on-device, and what "cleanup" means

It isn't really saturated. Two leaders take most of the spending, the features are crowded, and users are still unhappy.

LayerStateSo what
SpendConcentratedSpeechify takes about two-thirds to three-quarters; ElevenReader is #2. They own the ad auctions.
FeaturesSaturatedDocs import, resume, sync and good voices each ship in 3+ apps.
SatisfactionOpenAn F rating at the BBB over billing, "loses its place" reviews, a decade of iOS bug threads.
  • Why the niche existsIt's small ($5–16M), so it's not worth the leaders' time. Their models favor mass-market users and cloud voices. Getting document structure right is dull work that doesn't make a good ad.
  • Honest caveatThe main reason it's open is that it's small, and that same fact caps what you can earn.

The voice is cheap. Finding customers is what's expensive. Here's where $139 goes, as a lemonade stand.

Bars are scaled to one $139 year. Ad cost per payer can run past a whole year of revenue (the bar is clipped at $139), which is why these apps want a big payment up front.

  • Why so priceyAn install bought through ads costs $4–6, and only 2–10% of installs pay. Productivity apps renew at about 23%, so an app has to collect a big payment up front before the customer leaves.
  • Why they canBuyers compare against Read&Write (~$170/yr), Kurzweil (~$500/yr) and UK disability grants (DSA) that pay for the software.
  • Speech Central$9.99 works because of $0 voices (the phone's own), $0 ads and a tiny team.
  • Ads offset?Only about $0.01–0.05 per listening hour, because the screen is locked. That covers an on-device or self-hosted voice, but not a cloud voice at $0.77–1.54/hour. Recommendation: no ads.

Keeping documents on the phone works, and no iPhone limit prevents 2 hours of audio. Your Google Docs experience matches the evidence.

  • Google Docs"Listen" is for paid Workspace and AI Pro users, desktop web, English only. Free Gmail and the Docs iOS app get none, and the old "Speak selection" needs a screen reader on. Google's Gemini speech service caps each request at about 10.9 minutes, so a tool built on it that doesn't split text stops at about 10 minutes.
  • 2 hoursNo hardware limit: 2 hours is about 58 MB. Apple's built-in readers stop because of bugs. A dedicated app with background audio and sentence-by-sentence splitting can play for hours.
  • Private flowGoogle Docs app → "Send a copy" as PDF or Word → Share Sheet → cleaned and voiced on the phone. There's no server, and the App Store privacy label reads "Data Not Collected."
  • CatchiOS blocks GPU use in the background, so a Kokoro voice stops generating when the screen locks. Render ahead (2 hours takes ~36 min on a 13 Pro, ~7 min on a 16 Pro) or fall back to Apple voices. Whether the Neural Engine can keep going is unverified.

A PDF is a picture of pages, not a script. Cleanup turns it back into the thing a person would actually read aloud.

"…the committee recommended that the agency. CONFIDENTIAL — DRAFT v3 — Page 12 of 48. Section 4 Findings. Twelve. Increase funding by fifteen percent three. In the second quarter the impor- tant…"
ProblemCleanup does
Headers, footers, page numbersSkip text that repeats on every page
Footnotes (³ read as "three")Drop the marker; read the note at the end of the section, or on tap
Two columnsRebuild the reading order
TablesRead row by row, or say "table skipped"
"impor- tant"Rejoin hyphenated words
HeadingsPause, add chapter markers for jumping around
Scans and screenshotsRecognize the text first (Apple Vision)

Word, Docs exports, plain text, EPUB and web pages already carry their structure. Cleanup matters most for PDFs and scans, which is what most professional briefings are.
